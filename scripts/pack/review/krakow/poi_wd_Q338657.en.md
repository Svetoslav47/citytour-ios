## teaser
Stone apostles on their plinths guard the Church of Saints Peter and Paul.
It is the first structure in Kraków designed entirely in the Baroque style.
Inside hangs the longest Foucault pendulum in Poland.
## full
You're standing before the Church of Saints Peter and Paul, on Grodzka Street.
This street is part of the Royal Route, used by Polish kings to reach Wawel Castle.
The church is the first structure in Kraków designed entirely in the Baroque style.
It was funded by King Sigismund Vasa for the Jesuit order, and it was built between 1597 and 1619.
Of all the historic churches of Kraków, it has the largest number of seats.

In front of the church, sculptures of the apostles stand on their plinths.
The originals were completed in 1722, and acid rain damaged them badly.
What you see today are contemporary copies, made of the same stone.
The facade is built of dolomite, and its very top carries the coat of arms of the Vasa dynasty.

Inside hangs the longest Foucault pendulum in Poland.
Every Thursday, it is demonstrated as a simple proof that the Earth rotates.
The pendulum weighs 25 kilograms, and its red laser marks a clock face on the floor.
In the basement beneath the floor of the church, the priest Piotr Skarga is buried.

Before you walk on, look again at the apostles, each on its own plinth.
