## teaser
This round brick fortress is the Barbican, built around 1498 to defend the city.
It is one of only three such outposts still surviving in Europe, and the best preserved.
## full
You're standing in front of the Barbican, a fortified outpost and a historic gateway into the Old Town.
It is a round brick structure, ringed by a moat and crowned with seven turrets.
Its walls are three metres thick, and they hold 130 embrasures.

The city built it around 1498, fearing an attack by the Ottoman Empire.
It is one of only three such outposts still surviving in Europe, and the best preserved.
A covered passage once linked it to the city walls, through Saint Florian's Gate.
It served as a checkpoint for everyone who entered the city.

Early in the 19th century, the Barbican was threatened with demolition.
In 1817, two senators of the Free City of Kraków convinced the Senate to preserve it.
Today it belongs to the Historical Museum of the City of Kraków, and you can tour its interior.

Before you move on, count its seven turrets.
