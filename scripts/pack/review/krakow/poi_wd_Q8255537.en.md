## teaser
Kanonicza Street means Canon Street, and the canons of the cathedral once lived in its houses.
Many of their Renaissance and Baroque houses survive to this day.
## full
You're walking along Kanonicza Street, a historic street of the Old Town.
Its name means Canon Street.
It is the oldest street in Kraków.
It was once part of a hamlet, connected with Kraków in 1401.

The houses along the street were home to the cathedral canons, the priests who managed the cathedral.
Many Renaissance and Baroque houses survive here, with decorated portals.

Number 1 is a palace, built in 1531 and 1532 by a canon who later became a bishop.
Number 3 was built in the second half of the fourteenth century, for young people studying at a nearby college.
Its fine stone window frames date from the sixteenth century, and its rich Baroque portal from the eighteenth.
Number 25, at the foot of Wawel hill, is the Długosz House, built in the 14th century.
It once housed a royal bathhouse.
From the first half of the 15th century, cathedral canons lived there, and the house owes its name to one of them, Jan Długosz.

One end of the street was once closed off by a gate, demolished when Kraków was modernised.

As you walk, look at the decorated portals of the old houses.
