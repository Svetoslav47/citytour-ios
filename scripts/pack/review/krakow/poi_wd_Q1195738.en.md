## teaser
Saint Andrew's Church, built between 1079 and 1098, is a rare surviving fortress church.
In 1241 it was the only church in Kraków to withstand the Mongol attack.
## full
You're standing at Saint Andrew's Church, one of the oldest buildings in Kraków.
It is a Romanesque church, built between 1079 and 1098 by a medieval Polish statesman.
It is also a rare surviving example of a fortress church, used for defence.

Along the lower part of its facade, look for the small openings.
They served as defensive windows, at a time when the church was a place of refuge.
In 1241 it was the only church in Kraków to withstand the Mongol attack.
It gave shelter to most of the people of the city.
At that time it was called the lower castle, to set it apart from the upper one on Wawel Hill.

From 1320, the church belonged to the Order of Poor Clares.
The Baroque domes on top of its two octagonal towers were added in 1639.
Inside, the Baroque decoration includes a pulpit in the shape of a boat.

Before you walk on, look again at the two towers.
Their Baroque domes contrast with the severe Romanesque form of the church below.
