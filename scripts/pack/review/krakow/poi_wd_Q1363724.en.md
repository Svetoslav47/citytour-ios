## teaser
Saint Florian's Gate is where the Royal Route begins.
Kings and princes, foreign envoys and coronation processions once entered the Old Town through this tower.
## full
You're standing at Saint Florian's Gate, one of the best-known Polish Gothic towers.
The tower is thirty-three and a half metres tall, and its Baroque metal helmet, made in 1660, adds another metre.

The tower was first mentioned in 1307.
It was part of a rampart built around the city after the Tatar attack of 1241, which destroyed most of Kraków.
At its height, the wall had 47 watchtowers and eight gates.
This is the only one of those gates that was not dismantled in the 19th century.

The Royal Route begins here.
Through this gate came kings and princes, foreign envoys and distinguished guests, parades and coronation processions.
They travelled up Saint Florian's Street to the Main Market Square, and on up Castle Street to Wawel Castle.
Today, you follow in their footsteps.

Before you walk on, look for the carvings on the tower.
One face bears a stone eagle, carved in 1882 to a design by the painter Jan Matejko.
The other face shows Saint Florian himself, in a relief from the 18th century.
