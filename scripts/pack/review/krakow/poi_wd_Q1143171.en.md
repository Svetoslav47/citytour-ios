## teaser
Saint Mary's Basilica is home to the trumpet call that sounds every hour from the taller of its two towers.
The tune breaks off in mid-stream.
Stop for a moment if you'd like the full story.
## full
You're standing before Saint Mary's Basilica, a Brick Gothic church at the corner of the Main Market Square.
It was built in the 14th century, on foundations from the early 13th century.

Every hour, day and night, a trumpet signal is played from the top of the taller of its two towers.
Listen to the end, because the plaintive tune breaks off in mid-stream.
The story behind it is of a trumpeter, shot in the throat while he sounded the alarm before a Mongol attack.
The first account of that story comes from an American novel of 1928, The Trumpeter of Krakow.
At noon, the call is broadcast live by Polish Radio, and it is heard across Poland and abroad.

The church is most famous for its wooden altarpiece, carved by Veit Stoss.
Some of its painted murals were designed by Jan Matejko, the leading history painter of Poland.
In 1978, the church became a UNESCO World Heritage Site, together with the Historic Centre of Kraków.

Before you walk on, look once more at the taller tower, and wait for the next call.
## deep
The church you see is not the first on this spot.
According to the chronicler Jan Długosz, the first church was founded in 1221 by the Bishop of Kraków.
That building was destroyed during the Mongol invasion of Poland.
Between 1290 and 1300, a new early Gothic church was built on the remaining foundations.
It was consecrated in 1320.

During the reign of Casimir the Great, the church was completely rebuilt, and tall windows were added.
In the first half of the 15th century, the northern tower was raised to serve as the watch tower for the entire city.
A gilded crown was placed on its helmet in 1666, and it is still there today.
At the end of the 15th century, the church received its sculptural masterpiece, the late Gothic altarpiece of Veit Stoss.
It stands behind the high altar, and it was carved between 1477 and 1489.
Veit Stoss was a German-born sculptor, who lived and worked in this city for over 20 years.
Today the altarpiece is a national treasure of Poland.

In 1536, King Sigismund declared that the sermons here should be given in Polish instead of German.
The large German community then moved its worship to the smaller Saint Barbara's church.

In the 18th century, the interior was rebuilt in the late Baroque style.
All 26 altars were replaced, and the walls were decorated with painting.
At the beginning of the 19th century, a cemetery near the basilica was closed and replaced by a public square.
Between 1887 and 1891, the church gained its neo-Gothic design, with murals painted and funded by Jan Matejko.
He worked with two other artists, the authors of the stained glass in the presbytery.

The basilica also served as a model for many churches built by the Polish diaspora abroad, among them two in Chicago.
Listen once more for the trumpet from the taller tower before you go.
