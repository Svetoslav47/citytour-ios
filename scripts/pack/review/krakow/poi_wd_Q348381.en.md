## teaser
This bronze figure is Adam Mickiewicz, the greatest Polish Romantic poet of the 19th century.
He had never been to Kraków, yet here he stands, in the Main Market Square.
## full
You're standing at the monument to Adam Mickiewicz, the greatest Polish Romantic poet of the 19th century.
It was unveiled on June 16, 1898, a hundred years after his birth, with his daughter and son present.

The sculptor, Teodor Rygier, was little known at the time.
His design won by popular demand, ahead of more than 60 artists, the painter Jan Matejko among them.
At the poet's feet are four groups of figures, for the Motherland, Science, Courage and Poetry.
The inscription on the pedestal reads: To Adam Mickiewicz, the Nation.

In 1940, the monument was destroyed by the Nazis.
Most of the figures were recovered in 1946, from a scrap metal heap in Hamburg.
The monument returned to the square in 1955.

Mickiewicz himself had never been to Kraków.
In 1890, his remains were brought here from Paris and laid to rest in a crypt under Wawel Cathedral.
Every year on Christmas Eve, the florists of Kraków decorate the monument with flowers.

Before you walk on, look at the four groups of figures around the base.
