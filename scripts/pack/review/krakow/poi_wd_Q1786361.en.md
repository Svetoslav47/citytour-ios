## teaser
This massive Gothic tower is all that remains of the old Town Hall, demolished in 1820.
It leans by 55 centimetres, the result of a storm in 1703.
## full
You're standing at the Town Hall Tower, the only remaining part of the old Town Hall.
The rest of the building was demolished in 1820, as part of a plan to open up the Main Square.
From the 14th until the early 19th century, the Town Hall was the administrative hub of the city, the seat of its council and mayor.

The massive Gothic tower was built of stone and brick at the end of the 14th century.
It stands 70 metres tall.
It also leans by 55 centimetres, the result of a storm in 1703.

In 1680, lightning set fire to its original Gothic helmet.
The royal architect then designed a new and imposing Baroque helmet, which survived only until 1783.
When it began to crumble, it was replaced by a smaller structure.

Its cellars once housed a city prison, with a medieval torture chamber.
At the top, an observation deck is open to visitors, with a panoramic view of the city.

Before you walk on, look at the entrance.
It is guarded by a pair of stone lions, under the original Gothic portal with the city coat of arms.

## drafting notes
Sources are adequate (en article 2,711 chars, plus the en summary of the Kraków Town Hall article). View hint changed from royal-route.json (look up at "the tower's slight lean, about 55 cm"): a 55 cm lean on a 70 m tower cannot be seen from the square, and "cm" is an abbreviation for TTS. Proposed instead: look level at the stone lions at the entrance (grounded in the quote above); an alternative is look up at the helmet on top. Left out on purpose: Piotr Beber, Archbishop Kajetan Sołtyk, Wiktor Zin and the incorrect 1960s bay windows, the lions' origin in Pławowice, the theatre stage in the basement (cut to keep the full script near 200 words), the radio-controlled clock (inside, not visible) and the 1967 conservation. The tower's three numbers (70 m, 55 cm, 1703) are kept in short separate sentences.
