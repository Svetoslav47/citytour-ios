## teaser
This small church of Saint Adalbert is one of the oldest stone churches in Poland.
It is older than the square around it, and its floor lies below the level of today's pavement.
## full
You're standing at the Church of Saint Adalbert, one of the oldest stone churches in Poland.
Its history goes back almost a thousand years, to the beginning of Polish Romanesque architecture.
Merchants travelling from across Europe came here to worship.

The church was built in the 11th century, and it is named after the martyred missionary Saint Adalbert.
It is older than the square itself, by nearly a century.
The square was laid out in 1257, and it is the biggest medieval market square in Europe.

Over the centuries, new pavement raised the level of the square by 2 to 2.6 metres.
That is why the floor of the church lies below the present level of the square.
In the 17th century, its walls were raised, and it was topped with a new Baroque dome.

The crypt holds a small museum of the history of the Market Square.
In the 1960s, the earliest church was discovered here, dating back to the times when Saint Adalbert lived in Kraków.

Before you walk on, notice how the door opens directly into the noise of the square.
