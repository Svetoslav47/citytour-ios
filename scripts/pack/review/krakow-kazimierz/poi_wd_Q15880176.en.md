## teaser
This is the old Town Hall of Kazimierz, once the seat of a town of its own.
The clock on its tower started running in 1875, and it still keeps time today.
## full
You're standing at the old Town Hall of Kazimierz, on what was once the market square of a separate town.
In 1335, King Casimir the Great granted a cluster of settlements on the right bank of the Vistula the rights of a town.
The town was named after its founder: Kazimierz.
Its plan set out a square market place, 195 metres on each side, almost as large as the Main Square of Kraków.

The first seat of the town council was a wooden building among the market stalls.
Building in brick began in 1414, and parts of that building survive on the ground floor and in the cellars.
After a fire in 1623, a new storey was added, crowned with a crenellated attic.

At the turn of the nineteenth century, Kazimierz lost its town rights and became a district of Kraków.
In 1806, the empty town hall was even put up for auction, to be torn down.
Later, the Jewish community took it over, and it housed a school for Jewish children.
Since 1947, the building has been home to the Ethnographic Museum.

A plaque on its wall recalls how Jewish refugees were welcomed to Poland in the time of King Casimir the Great.
Before you go, look up at the clock on the tower, running since 1875.
