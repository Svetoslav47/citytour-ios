## full
You're standing at Corpus Christi Basilica, the great Gothic church of Kazimierz.
King Casimir the Great founded it in 1335.
It was built in stages, from 1340 until about the middle of the 15th century.
It was meant as a monastery church, which explains its large plot and the old monastic cemetery beside it.
In 1404, King Władysław Jagiełło gave it to the Canons Regular of the Lateran.

In 1655, during the Swedish invasion known as the Deluge, soldiers robbed the church and wrecked its interior.
That is why so much of what you see inside is Baroque.
Look for the gilded high altar and the pulpit shaped like a boat, made in 1750.
Bartolommeo Berrecci, the Renaissance artist who designed Sigismund's Chapel at Wawel, is buried here.

The basilica also houses the largest organs in Kraków.
The main instrument was built between 1958 and 1963, using parts of an older organ from the 1770s.
The main organ stands in the choir, and the side organs in the chancel.

Before you go, look up at the tall Gothic walls of the basilica.
