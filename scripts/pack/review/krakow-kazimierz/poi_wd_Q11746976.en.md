## teaser
This tall church without a tower is Saint Catherine's, the church of the Augustinian friars.
In 1443, an earthquake brought down the vault of its nave.
## full
You're standing at the Church of Saint Catherine, known as the Augustinian Church.
It is a basilica with three naves, no tower and no transept.
Its stonework counts among the finest medieval stonework in Poland.

The king who founded Kazimierz brought the Augustinian friars here, and building began in 1342.
He wanted his new town to have a church as beautiful as the churches of Kraków next door.
The chancel was consecrated in 1378.

Then the ground shook.
In 1443, an earthquake brought down the vault of the nave, and it took until 1505 to rebuild it.
In 1786, another earthquake cracked the vault.
Soon after, the Austrians closed the friary and turned the church into an arms store, and later into a store for straw and hay.
In 1827, the city senate even planned to pull it down.
A committee for its renewal, set up in 1833, saved it.

Inside, the furnishings are modest, and that lets the clean lines of the architecture speak.
Before you go, look at the late Gothic porch, with its two richly carved stone portals.
