## teaser

## full
You're standing at Skałka, the Church on the Rock, on the Vistula south of Wawel.
Until the nineteenth century, this rock was part of the island city of Kazimierz.
Then the Old Vistula River was filled in, and the island joined the land.

By tradition, this is the place where Saint Stanislaus, Bishop of Kraków, was slain in 1079.
The order came from King Bolesław the Bold.
The king went into exile, and the slain bishop was later canonised.
Each newly elected King of Poland made a pilgrimage here on the eve of his coronation, as penance for that deed.

The first church here was Romanesque.
King Casimir replaced it with a Gothic church, and since 1472 the Pauline Fathers have kept it.
Between 1733 and 1751, the church received its Baroque decor.

The crypt beneath the church is a resting place for distinguished Poles.
Among them are the historian Jan Długosz, the poet and painter Stanisław Wyspiański, and the poet Czesław Miłosz.

Before you walk on, find the Well of Saint Stanislaus outside the church.
According to legend, the king threw the bishop's body into this well.
Water from the well flows from a fountain, for pilgrims to drink.
## deep
The crypt is one of the National Panthéons of Poland, alongside the Wawel Cathedral.
The composer Karol Szymanowski rests there, and so does the painter Jacek Malczewski.
Czesław Miłosz, poet, essayist and Nobel Prize recipient, was buried there too.

In 2008, the Pauline fathers added the open-air Altar of the Three Millennia.
Its statues represent seven important people in Polish history.
Among them are Saint Stanislaus himself, Jadwiga, the first female monarch of the Kingdom of Poland, and Pope John Paul II, once archbishop of Kraków.
Six of the seven are venerated as saints in the Catholic Church.
