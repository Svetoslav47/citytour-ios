## teaser
This is the Tempel Synagogue, a Reform synagogue completed in 1862.
It is still a place of worship, and a home of Jewish culture in Kazimierz.
## full
You're standing at the Tempel Synagogue, also called the Progressive Synagogue.
It is a Reform congregation, and its synagogue was built between 1860 and 1862.
At that time, Kraków was part of the Austro-Hungarian Empire.
The building follows the pattern of the Leopoldstädter Tempel in Vienna, with a tall central section and lower wings.

Inside, the walls are covered in dense patterns, painted in many colours, and in gold leaf.
Above the Aron Kodesh, the ark of the synagogue, rises a gold-leaf dome.
It recalls the dome over the Sigismund Chapel in Wawel Cathedral.

During World War II, the German Nazis desecrated the synagogue and used it to store ammunition.
After the war, it was used again for prayer, and regular prayers were held until 1985.
Gifts from donors around the world paid for a renovation, from 1995 until 2000.
The congregation is still active today, though formal prayers are held only a few times a year.
The synagogue is also a centre of Jewish culture, with concerts and meetings during the Kraków Jewish Culture Festival.

Look up at the tall central section of the building, between its lower wings.
