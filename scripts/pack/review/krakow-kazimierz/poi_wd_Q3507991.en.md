## teaser

## full
You're standing at the Kupa Synagogue, also known as the Synagogue of the Poor.
In 1495, King John Albert set this neighbourhood aside for the Jewish community, which was moved here from the Old Town.
The Jewish community of Kazimierz founded this synagogue in 1643.
A contribution of 200 zlotys from the Jewish goldsmiths' guild helped to complete it.
It was built in the Baroque style, with a square prayer hall inside.
One of its walls joins the remnants of the medieval town wall of Kazimierz.

The paintings inside date from the 1920s.
They show the holy places of Hebron, Tiberias and Jerusalem, biblical scenes, and verses from the Psalms.
The signs of the Zodiac are painted over the women's gallery.

The Nazis devastated the synagogue during World War II.
Shortly after the end of the war, a Polish mob burned it down during the Kraków pogrom.
It was later restored with great care.
Until 1991, the building was used for secular purposes.
Today it is a place for meetings, concerts and exhibitions, especially during the Jewish Culture Festival, and it has become the main synagogue of the Jewish community of Kraków.

Look at the place where the synagogue meets the remnants of the old town wall.

## drafting notes
Sensitive stop (docs/research/new-tours.md §2.2): the wartime devastation and the post-war Kraków pogrom are named plainly, in the source's words, without adjectives or speculation about causes or numbers. "Used for secular purposes" renders the source's "profane purposes" (the everyday sense of profane would mislead a listener). Left out: the 19th-century annexes, the register number, Noah's Ark and the unidentified artist. The source says the wall is the northern wall; the script avoids the compass direction. The source's tense for the paintings is mixed ("was decorated", "are painted"); the script describes them as present, as the article's Zodiac sentence does. The en article says the building "has subsequently operated as a Jewish museum since 1996"; the pl article (more detailed) says it belongs to the Jewish community, which restored it from 1995, and has become the community's main synagogue, so the script follows the pl article for the present use. The pl article also describes the 1945 events differently (looted on 11 August 1945 during anti-Jewish riots, set on fire the next day, the fire put out); the script keeps the en wording "burned down by a Polish mob during the Kraków pogrom". Reviewer: please reconcile both points.
