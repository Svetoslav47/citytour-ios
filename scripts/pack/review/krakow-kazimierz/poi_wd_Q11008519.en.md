## teaser
This is Plac Nowy, the square with the round market hall in the middle.
The hall was built in 1900, and in 1927 it became a ritual poultry slaughterhouse for the Jewish community.
## full
You're standing on Plac Nowy, the New Square of Kazimierz.
Until the nineteenth century, a cluster of lanes and buildings stood here.
The square took its present shape in town plans of 1808 and 1844.
One side of it follows the line of the old defensive walls of Kazimierz.
The houses along that side once belonged to the Jewish hospital of the Kupa Synagogue.

In the middle stands the round market hall, built in 1900.
People call it the Okrąglak, the round one.
An inner hall is ringed by small shops on a twelve-sided plan, with an open-air passage in between.
In 1927, it was leased to the Jewish community, and it became a ritual poultry slaughterhouse.
After the war, it served as a market hall again.

On weekdays the square is still a market, for vegetables and for antiques.
It is also a place for festivals, among them concerts of the Jewish Culture Festival on the roof of the round hall.

Look at the round hall, and at its two entrances facing each other across it.
