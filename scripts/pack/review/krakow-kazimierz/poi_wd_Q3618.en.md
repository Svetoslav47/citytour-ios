## teaser
This is the Remuh Synagogue, an active Orthodox synagogue, completed in 1557.
Beside it lies the Old Jewish Cemetery, one of the oldest existing Jewish cemeteries in Poland.
## full
You're standing at the Remuh Synagogue, an Orthodox congregation and synagogue that is still in use.
It is named in honour of Rabbi Moses Isserles, known by the Hebrew acronym Remah.
He is famed for his commentaries and additions to Rabbi Yosef Karo's Shulchan Aruch.

The year 1552 was a very hard time for the family of Israel ben Josef, a wealthy banker.
His mother, his wife and his daughter-in-law died in the epidemic that hit Kraków that year.
The foundation tablet says that he built this synagogue from the bequest of his wife, Malka.
It was built at the edge of a newly established Jewish cemetery.
A first building burned in April 1557, and a masonry synagogue was raised in its place that same year.

During the Holocaust, the German Trust Office seized the synagogue.
It was stripped of its ceremonial objects and historic furnishings, and used as a store for firefighting equipment.
The building itself was not destroyed, and in 1957 it underwent a major restoration.

Beside the synagogue lies the Old Jewish Cemetery, established between 1535 and 1551.
During the German occupation, the Nazis tore down its walls and hauled away its tombstones as paving stones.
The tombstone of Rabbi Moses Isserles is one of the few that remained intact.
## deep
The cemetery is also known as the Remah Cemetery, after Rabbi Moses Isserles, who is buried there with his family.
It was closed around 1850, and the New Jewish Cemetery on Miodowa Street became the city's Jewish burial ground.
Izaak Jakubowicz, the donor of the Izaak Synagogue, is buried here too.
So is Rabbi Yom-Tov Lipmann Heller, a Talmudist best known for his commentary on the Mishnah.

After the war, the cemetery went through a series of restorations.
All the original tombstones found as paving stones were returned and set up again.
They are only a small fraction of the monuments that once stood here.
