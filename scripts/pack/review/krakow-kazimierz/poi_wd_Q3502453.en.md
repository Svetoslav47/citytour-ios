## teaser

It was the main religious, social and organisational centre of the Jewish community of Kraków.
## full
You're standing at the Old Synagogue on Szeroka Street, the Wide Street.
It is the oldest synagogue building still standing in Poland.
Sources differ on when it was first built, in 1407 or in 1492.
In 1570, the Italian architect Mateo Gucci rebuilt it.
He gave it an attic wall with loopholes, windows high above the ground, and thick walls with heavy buttresses.
These features were borrowed from military architecture, to withstand a siege.
It is a rare surviving example of a Polish fortress synagogue.

It was the main religious, social and organisational centre of the Jewish community of Kraków.
In 1794, General Tadeusz Kościuszko spoke here to gain Jewish support for his uprising.
A plaque in the entrance hall commemorates that day.

During World War II, the Germans devastated and ransacked the synagogue, looted its art, and used it as a warehouse.
In 1943, 30 Polish hostages were executed at its wall.
A memorial stone on this street is dedicated to the 65,000 Polish citizens of Jewish origin from Kraków and its surroundings who were murdered by the Germans.

Since 1958, the synagogue has been a branch of the Historical Museum of Kraków, with a focus on the Jews of Kraków.
Every year, the final concert of the Jewish Culture Festival takes place on Szeroka Street.
Before you leave, look up at the attic wall with its loopholes.
## deep
Szeroka Street was once the centre of the village of Bawół, which joined the town of Kazimierz in 1340.
From the end of the 15th century, Jewish families began to settle around it.
Over time a separate district grew up here, with its own city walls.
In the 19th century, most wealthier residents moved to other districts, and the area became home to the Jewish poor.

During the war, the synagogues were devastated, and the Jewish population was relocated to the ghetto in Podgórze.
After the war, the synagogues were rebuilt, except the Na Górce Synagogue.
In front of the Remuh Synagogue there is a memorial bench honouring Jan Karski.
The house at number 14 is the birthplace of Helena Rubinstein.

Inside the Old Synagogue, the museum's exhibits follow the stages of life: birth, prayer, diet, divorce and death.
The women's prayer room dates back to the 17th century.
