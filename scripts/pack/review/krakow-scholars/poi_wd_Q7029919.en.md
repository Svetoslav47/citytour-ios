## teaser
Here stands Nicolaus Copernicus, the astronomer who studied at the Kraków Academy.
His statue first stood in the courtyard of the Collegium Maius, and it came to the Planty in 1953.
## full
You're standing at the monument to the astronomer Copernicus, who studied at the Kraków Academy.
His father came from Kraków, which was then the capital of Poland.
The sculptor Cyprian Godebski designed the statue in 1899, and it was completed in 1900.

The idea came from an art historian, who also suggested showing the astronomer as a student of the university.
The statue was unveiled on 8 June 1900, during the celebrations of the university's jubilee.
For half a century it stood in the courtyard of the Collegium Maius, facing the main entrance of the college.

In 1953 it was moved here, to the Planty Park, in front of the Collegium Witkowski building.
The move was pushed by a professor who thought little of the statue's artistic value.
It caused great controversy, and some lecturers of the university protested.
This spot was first meant to be temporary, but in the end the statue stayed.

Before you walk on, look up at the young astronomer, shown as a student.
