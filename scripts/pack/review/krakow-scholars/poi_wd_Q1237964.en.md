## teaser
The Dominican church of the Holy Trinity has a history dating from the year 1223.
Saint Hyacinth of Poland is buried inside.
## full
You're standing at the Church of the Holy Trinity, known to locals as the Dominicans Church.
Built in the Gothic style, it also houses a monastery of the Order of Preachers.

The Dominicans came to Kraków in 1222, with Saint Hyacinth at their head.
The bishop of Kraków gave them the old parish church of the Holy Trinity, and it was consecrated in 1223.
They began to build the new Gothic church and monastery after an invasion in 1241.

Saint Hyacinth is buried here, and so is the Polish ruler Leszek the Black, who died in 1288.
In the chancel lies the bronze tomb plate of the humanist Filip Kallimach, who died in 1496.
It was made to a design by Wit Stwosz.

A free-standing brick bell tower once stood in front of the church.
The great city fire of 1850 left only its burnt walls, and they were taken down.
The same fire burned out the whole interior, except some chapels, and the vault of the nave collapsed.
Rebuilding took decades, and in 1884 the church was consecrated again.

Before you walk on, look at the Gothic portal of the main entrance, sheltered by a later porch.
