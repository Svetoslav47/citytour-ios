## teaser
This is the Collegium Iuridicum, one of the oldest buildings of the university.
Behind its gate lies a courtyard with two storeys of arcades.
## full
You're standing at the Collegium Iuridicum, one of the oldest buildings of the university.
Long before the college, a trading house stood on this spot, and its remains can still be seen in the cellars.
The university bought two town houses here in 1403 and 1406, and joined them together.
Many Gothic portals and window frames have survived.

Besides the Collegium Maius, lectures were once held in four colleges, the centres of the faculties of law, medicine, theology and the liberal arts.
Of these, only the Collegium Iuridicum, the law college, and the Collegium Minus survive.

Later the building was rebuilt, with the arcaded courtyard and a rusticated portal on the front.
From 1800 it housed a tax office, and later a military hospital.
From 1850 to 1886, the Collegium Iuridicum was the seat of the university.

Today it is home to the university's art historians and lawyers.
In the basement, a zoology exhibition shows sea shells of about 1500 species.
In 1996 a sculpture by Igor Mitoraj, a gift of the artist to the university, was placed in the courtyard.

Before you walk on, look through the gate at the two storeys of arcades around the courtyard.
