## teaser
Across the street from the Bishop's Palace stands the Franciscan church, which dates back to the 13th century.
Inside are stained glass windows by Stanisław Wyspiański.
## full
You're standing at the Franciscan Basilica, the church and friary of the Franciscan friars.
The church dates back to the 13th century.
It was one of the first tall brick and sandstone buildings in the city.
Its first Gothic structure was consecrated before 1269, and little remains of it but the ribbed vault.

The church lived through many disasters, but the most destructive fire came only in 1850.
The written records of its consecration were lost, along with priceless artifacts.

In 1895 Stanisław Wyspiański, the founder of Young Poland, painted murals with floral motifs in the church.
He was also the author of the magnificent stained glass windows in the apse.
They were made at a foundry in Innsbruck between 1899 and 1904.
The renovated church was consecrated again in 1908, and in 1920 it became a basilica.

In 1919, Saint Maximilian Kolbe was a friar here.
He led his first service in this church when Poland regained its sovereignty.

Before you walk on, look up at the tall brick walls, among the first of their kind in the city.
## deep
Historians do not agree on who founded the church.
Many regard Duke Bolesław the Chaste and his wife, Kinga, as its founders, for rebuilding it after the first Mongol invasion of Poland in 1241.
Around the year 1436 the cross-shaped central part was finished, and the church took the shape of a Greek cross for the first time.

The partitions of Poland were deadly for the Franciscan order.
By 1864, of over 90 Franciscan monasteries in the country only 8 remained, and the one in Kraków was among them.

Today the basilica keeps a certified copy of the Shroud of Turin, in the Chapel of the Passion.
The copy was consecrated by Pope John Paul II at the Vatican in 2003.
