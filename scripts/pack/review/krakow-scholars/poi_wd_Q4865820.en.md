## teaser
This is the Nowodworski school, one of the oldest secondary schools in Poland.
King Jan III Sobieski is on its list of former pupils.
## full
You're standing at the Bartłomiej Nowodworski Lyceum, one of the oldest secondary schools in Poland.
Its pupils know it as the Nowodworek.
On 5 May 1586, the Senate of the Jagiellonian University decided to found a school to prepare boys for the university.
The school opened in 1588.

Its patron, the nobleman and courtier Bartłomiej Nowodworski, endowed it in 1617 and 1619.
In recognition, the school became known as the Collegium Nowodworskiego.
In 1898 it moved from the university area to this building on Plac na Groblach.
Girls were admitted for the first time in 1962.

The list of its former pupils is long.
It includes Jan III Sobieski, King of Poland, the painter Jan Matejko and the writer Joseph Conrad.
It also includes Stanisław Wyspiański, whose stained glass fills the apse of the Franciscan church.

Every year before the Christmas break, the pupils stage a satirical nativity show that parodies their teachers.
It was first shown in 1963, at first as a secret from the teachers.

Before you walk on, look at the school building, a few hundred metres from Wawel Castle.
