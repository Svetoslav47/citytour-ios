## teaser
The Collegium Maius is the oldest building of the Jagiellonian University.
In the 1490s, a student named Nicolaus Copernicus studied here.
## full
You're standing at the Collegium Maius, the Great College, the oldest building of the Jagiellonian University.
It dates back to the 14th century.
King Władysław Jagiełło bought it for the Kraków Academy, with money left by his late wife, Queen Jadwiga.
At the time, the academy was the first university in Poland.

In the late 15th century it was rebuilt in the late Gothic style, around a large courtyard with arcades.
In 1517 a well was built in the middle of the courtyard.
Professors lived and worked upstairs, while lectures were held downstairs.

In the 1490s, Nicolaus Copernicus was among the students here.
He was the Renaissance astronomer who would change European ideas about the universe.
The statue of Copernicus on the Planty stood in this courtyard until 1953.

Today the building houses the Jagiellonian University Museum.
Its treasury holds the rectors' Gothic maces and the Jagiellonian globe.

Before you walk on, look into the courtyard with its arcades, where the professors once lived.
