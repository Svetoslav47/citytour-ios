## teaser
This is Saint Anne's, the university church, a leading example of Polish Baroque by Tylman van Gameren.
Its Gothic predecessor was pulled down because it had become too small.
## full
You're standing at the Church of Saint Anne, the collegiate church of the university.
It is one of the leading examples of Polish Baroque architecture, designed by Tylman van Gameren.
Yet the church's history goes back to the 14th century.

The church was first mentioned in 1381.
In 1407 a fire destroyed it completely, and King Władysław Jagiełło rebuilt it the same year in the Gothic style.
The king also bound the church to the university, giving it the right to name the parish priest.
In 1535 it was raised to the rank of a collegiate church.

In 1689 the Gothic church was pulled down, because it had become too small.
It could no longer hold the growing cult of Saint John Cantius, the patron saint of the university, who is buried here.
From 1689 to 1705 the new Baroque church was built, modelled on Sant'Andrea della Valle in Rome.
Its architect was a Dutchman who had become Polish, the chief architect of the royal court.

Inside, in the transept, you will find the tomb of John Cantius.

Before you walk on, look up at the Baroque church and picture the smaller Gothic one it replaced.
